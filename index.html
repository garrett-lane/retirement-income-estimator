About you

Investment accounts

Tax-deferred = 401(k), 403(b), traditional IRA. Tax-free = Roth. Taxable = brokerage, savings. Monthly contribution includes any employer match.

Contribution changes

Change an account's monthly contribution starting in a given month, once or every year in that month (for example, +$100 every January). Contributions stop at retirement.

One-time deposits & withdrawals

Bonuses, inheritances, or big purchases. Use a negative amount for a withdrawal. Amounts are in dollars of that month.

Spending in retirement

Spending changes

From a given age, spending becomes a new amount (for example, after the mortgage is paid off).

Income in retirement

Social Security, pensions, annuities, part-time work, rental income. Amounts are annual, in today's dollars. Income only counts once you retire, and any surplus is reinvested in your taxable account.

Assumptions

How taxes are applied
  • Tax-deferred: contributions and growth are untaxed; withdrawals are taxed at the after-retirement rate, so each withdrawal is enlarged to cover the tax.
  • Roth: withdrawals are tax-free.
  • Taxable: contributions are already-taxed dollars. If the box above is checked, growth is taxed as it's earned (a "tax drag"); withdrawals are then tax-free.
  • Retirement income marked taxable is taxed at the after-retirement rate.
  • Not modeled: required minimum distributions, tax brackets, Social Security's partial taxation, state-specific rules, early-withdrawal penalties.
